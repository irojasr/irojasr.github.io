📐 Tilings, Wolfram Rules & Mathematical Specifications
  • Regular Euclidean Tilings:
    • Square ($\{4, 4\}$): Each cell shares edges with 4 orthogonal neighbors (Up, Down, Left, Right).
    • Hexagonal ($\{6, 3\}$): Pointy-topped hexagons with 6 edge-sharing neighbors (odd-r offset layout).
    • Triangular ($\{3, 6\}$): Alternating Up ($\Delta$) and Down ($\nabla$) equilateral triangles sharing 3 edges with adjacent triangles.
  • Update Rule Formulation: A cell in state \(v\) transitions to its predecessor \(\text{pred}(v)\) at time \(t+1\) if and only if its neighborhood satisfies the active rule predicate for value \(\text{pred}(v)\).
  • Wolfram Parity (Rule 90/150 style): Target converts if the count of predecessor neighbors is odd (\(C_P \bmod 2 = 1\)). Even counts cancel out, generating self-similar fractal carpets.
  • Directional Alignments: Constrains updates along physical axes. Vertical requires both Up & Down neighbors to match; Horizontal requires Left & Right.
  • Simultaneous Execution: Transitions are strictly synchronous (\(A^{(t)} \mapsto A^{(t+1)}\)) using double buffering. Scan order does not bias wave propagation.
